Run a useful negotiation round
  1. Agree who is represented and how weights, scores, and costs were obtained. Presets contain synthetic assumptions.
  2. Set an exact threshold, group floors, a cost budget, and any non-negotiable clause choices.
  3. Save a named snapshot before changing assumptions. Undo restores up to 50 edits within this tab.
  4. Compare passing packages and inspect who loses support. Try a custom package and test downside support.
  5. Compare the working draft against an earlier snapshot. Take the changed assumptions back to participants.
  6. Export JSON for reopening, CSV for all input evidence, and a brief or printed readout for discussion. Print readout includes a discussion worksheet with unmarked option boxes. It is a worksheet, not a recorded vote. Human participants decide next steps.

Custom choices and support-drop settings are temporary analysis controls. JSON, CSV, and the Markdown brief describe the working proposal and optimized recommendation. Print includes the visible custom comparison, downside result, and discussion worksheet.

Passing packages

Try this option

Lock one alternative and re-run the solver on the remaining unlocked clauses. Review the preview before applying. Apply is an ordinary draft edit, so undo restores the previous locks.

Leave one group out

Each row omits that group's weight from the weighted average. Remaining weights are used as written, which renormalizes them. This is a sensitivity readout of the supplied scores, not a forecast of what people would do without that group.

Group contribution

Each group's pull on overall approval is its weight share times its average support. That is an accounting of the supplied scores, not bargaining power or a forecast of what a group could extract.

Try your own package

Choose one option per clause to inspect tradeoffs. This comparison does not change the draft, locks, or recommendation. Choices reset when you reload.

Stress-test the recommendation

Reduce every entered support score by the same number of percentage points, stopping at zero. Test the same package against the same threshold and group floors. Costs and locks stay fixed. This is a hypothetical downside scenario, not a forecast or a probability of consent.

Use this printed sheet to mark preferred options during conversation. It is a worksheet, not a recorded vote, legal ballot, or collective decision. Facilitator notes, veto highlights, and recommended package option labels from the inspected package are included when present.

Method

How it works

Count what changes. For each combination of one option per clause, the workshop computes a weighted average of the group support scores. It selects a passing combination by lowest change cost, then fewest changed clauses, then higher approval, then option ID order.

Only combinations meeting every support floor, cost budget, veto, and clause lock can pass. Near misses meet those constraints but miss the overall threshold.

The search is exhaustive only up to 50,000 lock-permitted combinations. Above that, it stops and tells you to reduce the choice set. The workshop also enumerates passing alternatives for comparison, even when the original already passes.

Limits

Keep human judgment in the room

Scores, weights, and costs are supplied by people. The workshop makes no semantic judgment about text, fairness, representation, or scope.

Small textual edits can have large semantic effects. A passing result is a deliberation prompt, not democratic legitimacy, consent, or authority to adopt a policy. This workshop does not provide legal advice.
