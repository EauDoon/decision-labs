How this pooled-purchase room works

  1. Pool buyers. Add private constraints: category, whole quantity, price ceiling, delivery limit, and accepted variants. Labels stay in this browser.
  2. Compare offers. Merchants set a price, minimum, capacity, and optional quantity ladder. The room shows which offer a cohort can actually unlock.
  3. Inspect who is excluded and why. Open the offer inspector for price, delivery, variant, budget, and capacity reasons. The merchant table still shows aggregates only.

This is a local planning aid, not a checkout. You can reopen this guide from the header.

03 / method

One transparent matching rule

An offer is compatible when its category and variant are accepted, its unit price is within the buyer's ceiling, and delivery is no later than the buyer's limit. Pickup offers still carry a stored shipping amount, but matching charges 0 shipping.

Whole buyer quantities are selected within merchant capacity. The offer unlocks only if those units meet its minimum. Qualified offers rank by fulfilled units, group headroom, buyers included, landed cost, then offer ID. Leftover whole orders may be shown against a second offer as a planning aid, not a dual checkout.

For quantity discounts, each price is tested inside its own unit range. The next tier's minimum ends the preceding band. Only actually allocated whole orders count toward a tier. The feasible band with the most units wins.

Limits

A simulator, not a marketplace

  • No real inventory, checkout, payments, identity, or delivery.
  • Maximum willingness to pay is not the same as realized consumer welfare.
  • Capacity allocation maximizes units for one offer, not fairness or savings across multiple offers.
  • Aggregates can still reveal information when a cohort is very small.
  • A real service would need merchant verification, buyer consent, dispute handling, and anti-manipulation controls.
