Four independent offline workbenches

Decision Labs

Make the assumptions visible before a high stakes conversation. Change one input, see who is affected, and carry the evidence into the decision.

Each workbench is a self-contained browser page. There is no shared account, hosted service, or model that claims to decide for you.

How it works

Each standalone page keeps the question, inputs, model, and outputs together so a reviewer can inspect one bounded case.

  1. Open a workbench and begin with its built-in synthetic scenario.
  2. Change one assumption at a time and read the constraints, alternatives, and review prompts around the result.
  3. Export JSON when a draft needs to move into a conversation. Exported data is the portable handoff; browser storage is not a backup.

Read the worked case in repository documentation for a short, end-to-end example.

Trust and limits

These workbenches make assumptions inspectable. People keep judgment, governing rules, and accountability.

  • Local first. Standalone pages run in the browser. The optional launcher binds to loopback and does not call a remote API or load live market, merchant, or account data.
  • Private by default. There is no sign-in or cloud save. Browser drafts stay on this device until you explicitly export or share them. Do not share drafts containing sensitive information.
  • Deterministic models. Valid inputs produce repeatable outputs. The models do not forecast, sample, or assign probabilities, prices, fairness, or legitimacy.
  • Not a decision maker. A ranked shock, pooled offer, clause package, or weekend queue is evidence for a conversation. It does not execute transactions.
  • Model notes. Each workbench documents its formulas, units, assumptions, and non-goals beside the tool. The catalog does not serve source or model files.
